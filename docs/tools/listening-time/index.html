How the numbers are worked out

Audiobook and podcast runtimes are always listed at normal speed (1x), so your real listening time is the remaining length divided by your playback speed. A book of 12 hours 30 minutes at 1.25x takes 10 hours (12.5 ÷ 1.25). The calculator subtracts your current position before dividing, so only the part you have not heard is sped up, and rounds the displayed duration to the nearest minute.

The finish date counts how many days you need at your daily listening budget, starting today, assuming you listen every day. If you pick a finish-by date, the calculator counts the days from today through that date, including both ends, and divides the book minutes left by your total listening minutes to find the speed that makes it fit. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you type leaves this page.

Worked examples

Every example below was run through this calculator in Chrome on 24 November 2026, and you can retype any of them into the form above to check the same numbers.

  • 45 minutes at 1.75x speed: enter 0 h 45 min with position 0 h 0 min and speed 1.75x. Dividing 45 by 1.75 gives 25.71 minutes, or 25 minutes and 43 seconds (saving 19 minutes and 17 seconds, or 43%). The calculator rounds this to 26 min.
  • 6-hour audiobook at 1.5x speed: enter 6 h 0 min at 1.5x. Dividing 360 minutes by 1.5 gives 240 minutes, or 4 h flat (2 hours saved, 33% shorter; 4 days at 60 minutes a day).
  • 12 h 30 min book from 3 h 15 min at 1.25x (default inputs): you are 26.0% through with 9 h 15 min (555 minutes) of book time left. At 1.25x that takes 7 h 24 min, which is 10 days at 45 minutes a day (10 h for the whole book).
  • 10-hour book from 2 h 0 min with a 7-day deadline at 45 min a day: 8 hours (480 minutes) remain over 315 minutes of daily listening across 7 days. Dividing 480 by 315 gives 1.52x, which the calculator shows when Finish by is set six days from today.

What a faster speed saves

Each step up saves a smaller slice than the one before it, which is why most listeners settle between 1.25x and 1.75x.

Questions

How long is 45 minutes at 1.75x speed?

45 minutes at 1.75x speed takes 25 minutes and 43 seconds (45 divided by 1.75 is 25.71 minutes), which saves 19 minutes and 17 seconds. The calculator rounds displayed times to the nearest minute, so it shows 26 min.

How do I work out how long an audiobook takes at 1.5x?

Divide the normal-speed length by 1.5. A 6-hour audiobook takes 4 hours and a 12-hour audiobook takes 8 hours; at 2x they take 3 hours and 6 hours.

Where do I find the position and the length?

Your player shows both. In ABS+ and other Audiobookshelf apps they sit on the player screen, and the Audiobookshelf web player shows them under the cover. Use the book's normal-speed length, not the adjusted time left.

Why does my player show a different time left?

Some players adjust the remaining time for your playback speed while others always show normal-speed time. This calculator expects normal-speed length and position and divides by your speed itself.

What speed do I need to finish a book by a date?

Divide the normal-speed minutes left in the book by the total minutes you plan to listen between today and your target date, counting both days. Pick a date in the Finish by field and the calculator works that speed out for you.

Listen on your own Audiobookshelf server

ABS+ is a small, free, open-source Android and iPhone player for your own Audiobookshelf server, with offline downloads, six saved playback speeds (0.8x, 1x, 1.25x, 1.5x, 1.75x and 2x) and progress that syncs back to the server.
